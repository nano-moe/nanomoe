Scaling hull attention: a first attempt

Christos' blog post showed you can embed a lossless interpreter inside a transformer. That relied on two things: a complicated compilation procedure, and fast attention. I hacked on scaling the fast attention part.

Demo Day · March 2026

The attention trade-off

Normal attention is like a human: you see with two eyes, but in very high resolution. Christos' hull attention is like a spider that got smacked in the face — many more eyes, but each one at much lower resolution. This likely has qualitatively different properties than normal attention.

What we already knew

Guoqing and I tested hull attention on normal language modeling. The performance was much worse than standard attention. Spider vision doesn't do well on tasks that need high-resolution token relationships.

A simpler domain: ListOps

Today I tried a simpler setting — ListOps from the Long Range Arena. Guess what a nested math expression evaluates to, without scratch space.

[MAX 4 3 [MIN 2 8] 1 ] → 4

Sequences go up to 2,000 tokens deep. No scratchpad. Just read and predict.

Results

Both models are equally bad. Neither cracks ListOps without scratch space. But the interesting thing: hull attention doesn't do any worse.

The speed gap

The other problem: hull is 15.6× slower end-to-end. The kernel was written for a small number of heads. At 64 heads the performance story inverts — it needs to be reworked for the many-eyes regime.

What this tells us

Spider vision works

On a task where neither approach succeeds, hull attention doesn't do worse. The many-eyes-low-resolution trade-off doesn't destroy information — at least not here.

Structured state tracking

Hull attention seems well-suited to tasks with many parallel states to track — brackets, scopes, nested operations. Many low-res eyes may beat few high-res ones when the job is tracking structure, not resolving fine detail.

What's next

1. Fix the kernel

Rework the kernel for the many-heads regime. The current impl assumes few heads.

2. More structured tasks

Code execution, nested scoping, multi-state tracking — lean into the spider's strengths.

3. Longer sequences

The geometric advantage should grow with length. Test it.
